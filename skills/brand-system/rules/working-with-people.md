# Working with the person

A brand belongs to the person who has to live with it. These rules come from doing this work for real, and each
one is here because ignoring it cost time or trust.

## Refine, do not replace

When someone brings a mark, a sketch or an idea, it is the starting point, not a brief to improve on by
substitution. Their bird stays their bird. What you fix is craft:

- curves that wobble, corners that are not quite corners, points that do nothing
- gaps that are uneven, or so thin that they close at small sizes
- parts that are nearly the same but not quite (make them the same, or clearly different)
- a mark that is fine at 500 px and a smudge at 24 px

Show the corrected mark beside the original, at the same size, and say in a line or two what changed. If you
believe a different design would serve them better, show it too, in the same sheet, labelled as your own idea.
Never as the main line, and never by quietly drifting their mark towards yours.

The same goes for what they tell you later. An idea of theirs ("only the wing should change colour") is worth more
than a cleverer one of yours, because they will defend it. Turn it into a rule the whole system follows.

## Show options when it is a matter of taste

You cannot know which blue they will like. Do not ask them to imagine it and do not pick for them.

- Build two to four **finished** options: the real lockup in the real colours, made by the engine, not a
  description and not a swatch.
- Put them **side by side on one sheet** (`run sheet`), each with a letter. Say where the sheet is, as a link they
  can click, and show the picture in the conversation if your tool can.
- Give your recommendation and the reason in a sentence or two. Then wait.
- When they choose, write it into the settings and the notes, and **remove the other options** with `run discard`.
  Stale options left lying around get mistaken for the real thing.

Do not show options for things that are not taste. Whether a banner is 2560 pixels wide is a fact. Just do it.

## Each brand is its own

Nothing is carried from one brand to the next because it was at hand: not the typeface, not the colours, not the
list of items. The samples in these rules show how settings are written, not what a brand should choose. If two
brands you made look alike, one of them did not get its own decisions.

## Gates

Five points where you stop and wait for a clear yes: the mark, colour, type, items, motion. Plus the final review
of the page. The reason is cost: everything after the mark is drawn from the mark, so a mark changed late means
everything is made again. Two gates may be shown together when the person finds that easier (colour with type,
say). They are still separate decisions, and each gets its own yes.

- A gate is passed when the person says so in their own words. Silence, "looks fine I guess" about something else,
  or approval of an earlier version is not a yes for this one.
- Write each approval into `brand/notes.md` with the date.
- A decision the person made is theirs. Do not reopen it later because you would have chosen differently. If you
  find a real problem with it (the colour fails contrast, the name collides with something), say what you found and
  let them decide again.

## Nothing goes live without their word

The skill makes files. It never uploads, posts, publishes, or changes an account, a website or a store.

- "Make the banner" is not "upload the banner". Approval of the design is not permission to publish it.
- If they ask you to put something live and your tools can do it, do one place at a time and confirm each public
  change at that moment: what will change, where, and that it is public.
- Never type a password, a code or a card number for them. They sign in themselves.
- Keep the Rollout view honest: it lists what is ready, and its ticks are for the person to set.

## Say what a long job will do before starting it

Rendering animations can take many minutes and makes the computer busy. Before a long step, say in a sentence or
two what it will make, how many files, and roughly how long. If the person is using the computer for other heavy
work, offer to wait. Render one animation first, look at it, and only then render the set.

## Report plainly

After each stretch of work, tell them:

- what was made, with the link to the page or the sheet
- what was tested and how (`run check` says it in sentences you can pass on)
- what failed, with what the tool printed
- what you could not verify, and where you looked. Platform picture sizes, for instance, are the ones in common
  use: they should be confirmed in each upload dialog. Whether a name or a mark is free to use is a question for a
  trademark search and, if it matters, a lawyer. Say so once, plainly. Do not pretend to have checked.

Write for someone who was not watching you work. Lead with the result. Skip the story of how you got there.

## Keep the notes

`brand/notes.md` is the memory of the project. A later session, yours or someone else's, starts by reading it.
Record what was asked, each decision and who made it, each approval, and what is open. Keep it short. When
something in it stops being true, change it.

## Clean up after yourself

- Options that lost go to the bin with `run discard`, not into a growing pile.
- Nothing is deleted outright. If you must remove something the person made, move it to the bin and say so.
- Scratch files of yours do not belong in the brand folder.
